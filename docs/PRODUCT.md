# Product direction · first browser releases

## The product

Focusaurus helps someone who works in a browser protect a chosen stretch of
attention. They choose their own distracting sites, start a session, and meet
Doug when the browsing reflex takes over. The interruption should feel useful,
brief, and kind enough that they keep the extension enabled tomorrow.

The user confirmed this release scope on September 8, 2026. The older plan to
make time tracking, budgets, streaks and Firefox prerequisites for 1.0 is retired.
A dependable focused product is the first release; additional mechanics need
their own product evidence.

## Audience and promise

For individual Chrome and Firefox desktop users who want a voluntary focus companion. It is not
parental control, employee monitoring, clinical treatment, or an unbreakable
restriction system. No medical or productivity-outcome claims are made.

Promise: **Make room for the thing you meant to do.**

Within a minute of installation, a user should be able to choose a category,
start a session, and understand what is being paused. There is no account or
mandatory tour. Category packs remain available after setup and are always
editable; no site is silently added later.

## Release behavior

| Moment | Expected behavior |
| --- | --- |
| First open | Meet Doug, select categories or enter a site, start a 25-minute session. Empty lists cannot start sessions. |
| Start focus | Install redirect rules and pause matching open tabs. Open tabs paused automatically do not count as attempts. |
| Reach for a site | Show the matched site, local-day attempt count, session time, and supportive rotating copy. |
| Need access | After the chosen pause, offer a bounded temporary pass. The worker enforces the wait. Locked mode removes passes; ending the session remains available in the popup. |
| Session ends | Release rules and offer onward navigation on any blocked page still open. |
| Work hours | Start and end automatically. Respect manual stops for the remainder of that window. Follow local time and support overnight shifts. |
| Return tomorrow | No accumulated guilt. History describes the behavior; Doug does not judge the person. |
| Review data | Show the past seven days of blocked attempts. Let users export settings, import deliberately, and delete history. |

## Tone and visual principles

Doug is on your side. No scolding, deficit framing, threats, or exaggerated
celebration. Frequent interruptions get softer, not harsher. The artwork follows
a warm illustrated field guide: tactile green and oat gouache, clay plates,
organic contours, calm typography, and light-touch motion.

Every expression is accompanied by an explanation. Color and mood cannot be
the only way to understand state. The toolbar uses a proportionally rescaled
happy Doug, matching the illustrations in the popup, welcome and blocked pages.
Both themes preserve Doug’s painted colors.

## What to learn after launch

These are hypotheses, not established market facts:

1. A friendly interruption can make a voluntary blocker easier to keep using.
2. Attempt counts can help people notice a habit without requiring time tracking.
3. Category packs can shorten setup without creating unwanted blocking.

Validate with a small opt-in pilot and conversations, not hidden telemetry:

- Can new users begin a useful session within 60 seconds?
- Do they understand whole-domain versus path blocking?
- Does a temporary pass work when they actually need it?
- Is the extension still enabled after two weeks, including a difficult day?
- Do they describe Doug as supportive or patronizing?

No competitor rankings, unsupported install counts, or promises of improved
mental health are used in the listing. Budgets should be explored only after
the core product earns trust.
